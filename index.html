ML Internship Research Artifact

Refresh / Content Opportunity Scoring

Using observed content-performance signals to prioritize content items for human review.

Author: Supreet Chinnannavar FlyRank ML Internship August 2026 Decision-support analysis

0. Abstract

This study asks whether observed content-performance signals can support prioritization of content items for human review. The analysis uses the FlyRank ML Internship dataset, using March 2026 performance signals as features and whether a content item received at least one Google Search Console click in April 2026 as the outcome. A Random Forest classifier using five observed search and Analytics features was evaluated against a transparent Week-4 rule-based baseline on the same held-out validation data. The Random Forest measured a ROC-AUC of 0.9312 and a PR-AUC of 0.8397, compared with 0.8447 and 0.4858 for the baseline, while accuracy was 0.8762 compared with 0.8325. The resulting ranked output is intended as directional decision-support for human review rather than as evidence that refreshing content will cause additional clicks.

1. Problem framing

The decision supported by this analysis is which content items should receive earlier human review for a possible refresh opportunity.

The unit of analysis is a client-content item pair observed over a monthly performance window. March 2026 performance signals are used as the feature window, and April 2026 Google Search Console clicks are used as the outcome window.

The model produces a ranked decision-support output that can help prioritize content items for review.

A human reviewer can use the ranking to decide which content items to inspect first. The reviewer should then consider the actual content, search intent, search-result context, seasonality, data quality, and other relevant editorial context.

The cost of a wrong call is primarily inefficient use of editorial review and content resources. A false positive may cause time to be spent reviewing a page that does not represent a useful opportunity, while a false negative may cause a potentially useful review candidate to receive less attention.

Intended decision: prioritize content items for human review rather than automate content changes.

Excluded fields

Client and content identifiers were excluded from model features. Other warehouse fields were excluded because they were not required for the final feature vector, could represent availability metadata, could duplicate other measures, or were not appropriate predictive inputs for this task.

Future outcome information was not included in the model feature set. Label-derived fields such as trend_direction and trend_pct were also treated as inappropriate predictive inputs because they could encode information derived from outcomes or future changes.

Data safety: no client names, private queries, or directly identifying client information are included in this public research artifact.

3. Methodology

Target definition

The target is defined as: April 2026 Google Search Console clicks > 0.

Model

A Random Forest classifier was used because the task involves a binary outcome and potentially nonlinear relationships between observed performance signals.

Baseline

The Week-4 baseline identifies a potential refresh opportunity when impressions are at least 100 and CTR is below 2%.

Validation design

March 2026 was used as the feature window and April 2026 as the outcome window. The resulting modeling frame was divided into training and held-out validation data using an 80/20 stratified split with random_state = 42.

The split was stratified rather than client-grouped. Therefore, the evaluation should not be interpreted as testing generalization to completely unseen clients.

Leakage control

Future target information and client/content identifiers were excluded from the model feature set. Additional leakage checks found no obvious future-window, target-derived, product-flag, or label-derived features.

Target base rate

The evaluation contained 331,436 observations. There were 62,098 positive targets and 269,338 negative targets.

The positive target rate was 18.74%, while the negative rate was 81.26%. Accuracy should therefore not be interpreted without considering class imbalance.

Error analysis

The Random Forest confusion matrix was:

Predicted 0 Predicted 1
Actual 0 47,577 6,291
Actual 1 1,918 10,502

False-positive examples included pages with substantial impressions and clicks but no positive April outcome. This shows that strong historical search visibility does not guarantee the defined future outcome.

False-negative examples included pages with little or no March search activity that nevertheless received many April clicks. This illustrates that future demand can change and may not always be represented by the previous month's signals.

5. Limitations & honest framing

  • The model measures association between historical performance signals and the defined future outcome; it does not establish causal impact.
  • The evaluation used a stratified 80/20 split rather than a client-grouped split, so generalization to completely unseen clients has not been established.
  • The target is whether April received at least one click. It does not measure click growth, traffic lift, revenue, or the quality of a content refresh.
  • Search intent, seasonality, SERP features, content quality, and other contextual factors are not fully represented by the five model features.
  • Feature importance indicates model usage of a feature; it does not demonstrate that changing that feature would cause the outcome to change.
  • The results should be treated as measured and directional, rather than as a production guarantee.
Important: This work does not claim to predict Google's algorithm and does not claim that refreshing content will cause additional clicks.

6. Ranked recommendations

The Week-7 action playbook provides a transparent rule-based decision-support queue. It is separate from the Random Forest evaluation and is intended to provide an interpretable starting point for human review.

Priority 1 — REFRESH review

Items with at least 100 impressions and CTR below 2% receive the reason code:

HIGH_IMPRESSIONS_LOW_CTR

The queue contains 100,660 such items, representing 30.37% of the 331,437 content items.

Priority 2 — MONITOR

Items that do not satisfy the refresh rule receive:

LOW_SEARCH_SIGNAL

There were 230,777 such items, representing 69.63% of the queue.

Human review requirements

Before taking action, an editor should check search intent, relevance, current search-result context, content quality, seasonality, recent changes, data quality, and other relevant editorial context.

No-go cases

The following should not be automated from this analysis:

  • Publishing content
  • Deleting content
  • Redirecting URLs
  • Changing metadata
  • Rewriting content
  • Other irreversible content changes

The final content decision remains a human decision.
