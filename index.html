0. Abstract

This study examines whether observed content-performance signals can support the prioritization of content items for human review. The analysis uses the FlyRank ML Internship dataset, with March 2026 performance signals used as features and whether a content item received at least one Google Search Console click in April 2026 used as the outcome. A Random Forest classifier using five observed search and Analytics features was evaluated against a transparent rule-based baseline on the same held-out validation data. The Random Forest measured a ROC-AUC of 0.9312 and a PR-AUC of 0.8397, compared with 0.8447 and 0.4858 for the baseline, while accuracy was 0.8762 compared with 0.8325. The resulting ranked output is intended as directional decision-support for human review and not as proof that refreshing content will cause additional clicks.

1. Problem framing

The decision supported by this analysis is which content items should receive earlier human review for a possible refresh opportunity.

The unit of analysis is a client-content item pair observed over a monthly performance window. March 2026 performance signals are used as the feature window, and April 2026 Google Search Console clicks are used as the outcome window.

The model produces a ranked decision-support output that can help prioritize content items for review.

A human reviewer can use the ranking to decide which content items to inspect first. The reviewer should then consider actual content, search intent, search-result context, seasonality, data quality, and other relevant editorial context.

The cost of a wrong call is primarily inefficient use of editorial review and content resources. A false positive may cause time to be spent reviewing a page that does not represent a useful opportunity, while a false negative may cause a potentially useful review candidate to receive less attention.

Intended decision: prioritize content items for human review rather than automate content changes.

Excluded fields and safety decisions

Client and content identifiers (client_hash_id and content_hash_id) were excluded from model features because they are identifiers, not meaningful performance signals. They are used only for grouping and identifying rows within the internal workflow.

Data-availability fields such as client_has_gsc, client_has_ga4, gsc_data_available, and ga4_data_available were excluded because they describe availability of measurement rather than the intended performance signals.

Additional warehouse metrics, including gsc_sum_position, session-level aggregates, traffic-channel fields, AI-source fields, scroll_events, report_date, and month, were excluded from the final model feature vector because they were not part of the final five-feature specification.

Future outcome information was not included in the model feature set. Label-derived fields such as trend_direction and trend_pct were treated as inappropriate predictive inputs because they could encode information derived from outcomes or future changes.

Public-safety check: no client names, private queries, or directly identifying client information are included in this public research artifact.

3. Methodology

Target definition

The target is defined as: April 2026 Google Search Console clicks > 0.

Model

A Random Forest classifier was used because the task involves a binary outcome and potentially nonlinear relationships between observed performance signals.

Model features

  • gsc_impressions
  • gsc_clicks
  • gsc_avg_position
  • ga4_pageviews
  • ga4_engaged_sessions

Baseline

The Week-4 baseline identifies a potential refresh opportunity when impressions are at least 100 and CTR is below 2%.

Validation design

March 2026 was used as the feature window and April 2026 as the outcome window. The resulting modeling frame was divided into training and held-out validation data using an 80/20 stratified split with random_state = 42.

The split was stratified rather than client-grouped. Therefore, the evaluation should not be interpreted as testing generalization to completely unseen clients.

Leakage control

Future target information and client/content identifiers were excluded from the model feature set. Label-derived and future outcome fields were not used as predictive features.

Random Forest configuration

n_estimators 200
max_depth 10
min_samples_leaf 20
class_weight balanced

Measured improvement

  • ROC-AUC improvement: +0.0865
  • PR-AUC improvement: +0.3539
  • Accuracy improvement: +0.0437

Target base rate

The evaluation contained 331,436 observations. There were 62,098 positive targets and 269,338 negative targets.

The positive target rate was 18.74%, while the negative rate was 81.26%. Accuracy should therefore not be interpreted without considering class imbalance.

Interpretation of the metrics: ROC-AUC and PR-AUC provide more useful discrimination information than accuracy alone for this imbalanced target. The Random Forest measured higher ROC-AUC and PR-AUC than the transparent baseline on the same held-out validation data.

Error analysis

The Random Forest confusion matrix was:

Predicted 0 Predicted 1
Actual 0 47,577 6,291
Actual 1 1,918 10,502

The confusion matrix shows both false positives and false negatives. False positives are observations predicted as positive where the April outcome was zero, while false negatives are observations where the model predicted the negative class despite the presence of at least one April click.

These errors show that March performance signals do not perfectly determine the subsequent April outcome. The model therefore provides prioritization support rather than certainty about future content performance.

5. Limitations & honest framing

  • The model measures association between historical performance signals and the defined future outcome; it does not establish causal impact.
  • The evaluation used a stratified 80/20 split rather than a client-grouped split, so generalization to completely unseen clients has not been established.
  • The target is whether April received at least one click. It does not measure click growth, traffic lift, revenue, or the quality of a content refresh.
  • Search intent, seasonality, SERP features, content quality, and other contextual factors are not fully represented by the five model features.
  • Feature importance, if inspected, indicates model usage of a feature; it does not demonstrate that changing that feature would cause the outcome to change.
  • The results should be treated as measured and directional, rather than as a production guarantee.
Important: This work does not claim to predict Google's algorithm and does not claim that refreshing content will cause additional clicks.

6. Interpretation

The main measured result is that the Random Forest separated the defined future-click outcome more effectively than the Week-4 rule baseline on the held-out validation data.

The ROC-AUC increased from 0.8447 for the baseline to 0.9312 for the Random Forest, while PR-AUC increased from 0.4858 to 0.8397. This indicates stronger measured ranking performance for the model under the evaluation setup used here.

The model uses five observed performance signals: impressions, clicks, average position, pageviews, and engaged sessions. These signals provide different views of historical search visibility and engagement, allowing the Random Forest to capture relationships that a single transparent threshold rule cannot represent.

A key limitation of this interpretation is that the model's performance does not demonstrate that any individual feature causes future clicks. The analysis identifies predictive association within the observed data and should therefore be used as decision-support.

Observed finding: the Random Forest produced stronger measured discrimination than the Week-4 rule baseline under the same held-out validation setup.

7. Ranked recommendations

The Week-7 action playbook provides a transparent rule-based decision-support queue. It is separate from the Random Forest evaluation and is intended to provide an interpretable starting point for human review.

Priority 1

REFRESH review

Items with at least 100 impressions and CTR below 2% receive the reason code:

HIGH_IMPRESSIONS_LOW_CTR

The recommendation queue contains 100,660 items satisfying this rule, representing 30.37% of the 331,437 ranked items.

Priority 2

MONITOR

Items that do not satisfy the refresh rule receive:

LOW_SEARCH_SIGNAL

There were 230,777 such items, representing 69.63% of the recommendation queue.

Human review requirements

Before taking action, an editor should check search intent, relevance, current search-result context, content quality, seasonality, recent changes, data quality, and other relevant editorial context.

No-go cases

The following should not be automated from this analysis:

  • Publishing content
  • Deleting content
  • Redirecting URLs
  • Changing metadata
  • Rewriting content
  • Other irreversible content changes

The final content decision remains a human decision.
